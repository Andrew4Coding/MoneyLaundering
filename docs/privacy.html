Swiftlet Privacy Policy

Last updated 9 September 2026

Swiftlet is a personal expense tracker for iOS, developed by Andrew Devito Aryo ("we", "us"). This policy explains what the app stores and where it goes. The short version: your financial data stays on your device and, if you choose to sign in, in your own private iCloud. We do not run servers that receive it, and we do not sell or share it with anyone.

What the app stores

  • Transactions you enter: amount, type (expense or income), category, note, date, and money source.
  • Receipts and bills: photos you take or import, the line items read from them, and the text extracted for parsing.
  • Sign in with Apple details, if you use it: the user identifier Apple provides, and the name you allow Apple to share on first sign-in. These are kept in the app's local settings on your device and are used only to keep you signed in.

Where it is stored

  • By default everything is stored locally on your device. The app works fully offline and does not require an account.
  • If you sign in with Apple, your transactions, categories, and bills are synced through your own private iCloud database (Apple CloudKit) so they appear on your other devices. This data is held in your personal iCloud account under Apple's terms. We cannot read it.

On-device processing

Natural-language transaction entry and category suggestions are handled by Apple's on-device models on supported devices. The text you type for these features is processed on the device and is not sent to us.

What we do not do

  • No analytics, advertising, or tracking SDKs are included in the app.
  • No third-party services receive your data.
  • We do not have a server that stores your transactions or receipts.

Camera and photo access

The app asks for camera and photo library access only so you can attach a picture of a bill to a transaction. Photos you attach are stored with that transaction as described above.

Data retention and deletion

Data stays until you delete it. Removing a transaction, bill, or category deletes it from the device and from your iCloud database on your next sync. Deleting the app removes its local data; to also clear synced data, delete it in the app first or remove Swiftlet data from iCloud in iOS Settings. You can stop syncing at any time by signing out.

Children

Swiftlet is not directed at children under 13.
